\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have proposed a pricing test for training objectives motivated by a change
in a statistic of their output. The change the trained objective
achieves is imposed on the baseline's own samples, and its value $V$ and the
mechanism residual gap split the observed difference exactly,
$\mathrm{MRG} = D + V$. Positive and null controls check the verdict's
direction and this accounting, and a planted-mechanism control, which acquires
AWWL's change through training alone, returns an MRG indistinguishable from
zero, so for that change a filter-made and a training-made change are valued
alike. For AWWL on CIFAR-10, the
spectral change is worth \priceTransplantValue~FID, mostly in radii 7--14,
not in the outer radii where the deficit is largest, and AWWL trails
the baseline carrying that change by $\mathrm{MRG} = \mrgMain$~FID (raw
$p = \mrgMainP$), a result that does not survive Holm correction across the
\nFamilyPricing{} priced arms ($p = \mrgArmAWWLHolm$). Trained without the
other, AWWL's timestep schedule and frequency shape at constant total
weight $S$ are each worse than MSE (by \dTimestep{} and \dSumFixed~FID) and
partly cancel when combined: adding the shape to the schedule changes FID by
$\dAWWLVsTimestep$ (raw and Holm $p = \dAWWLVsTimestepP$), adding the
schedule to the shape by $-\dSumFixedVsAWWL$
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = \dSumFixedVsAWWLHolm$). The split of AWWL's cost thus
depends on the order, while the spectral change comes mainly from the shape;
as fixing $S$ does not fix the effective timestep weight $\Lambda(t)$, the
interaction is described, not tested. Across arms, corrected MRG and FID-gap
verdicts coincide, so the test adds the decomposition: what each change is
worth and, for AWWL, in which band. In DINOv2 features AWWL's gap to MSE is
resolved and its spectral change is worth nothing, so the verdict does not
rest on Inception features. The price of the same spectral correction changes sign on CelebA-64,
which differs from CIFAR-10 in data, resolution, operating point and training
steps. The significant MRG verdicts on trained objectives
rest on one small 200-epoch CIFAR-10 recipe (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

Two practices follow. The value of a candidate change needs only a baseline's
samples, so it can be computed for a new dataset or metric before any
objective is trained, and shows where the metric rewards the statistic.
Filtered samples should be scored without re-quantisation, or with a
white-noise control, because noise within half a quantisation step lowers
the FID of the baseline samples studied. The construction applies to any
objective whose stated mechanism is a statistic of finished samples that an
operator can set, such as a colour histogram or edge magnitudes, scored by
any metric that registers it (Section~\ref{sec:discussion-use}); testing it
beyond radial spectra comes next.
