\section{Experimental protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The protocol is stated in full because the study's conclusions are negative,
and a negative result is only as good as the reader's ability to see what would
have shown a positive one.

\subsection{Replication and variance}

Every configuration is trained with five seeds. We report mean $\pm$ standard
deviation with a \SI{95}{\percent} confidence interval, and compare with a
paired-by-seed $t$-test under Holm--Bonferroni correction across the comparison
family. The seed controls initialisation, data order and sampling noise
together, so a pair of runs differs in everything a practitioner would leave to
chance.

Pairing matters more than the test. The standard deviation of FID across five
seeds of an identical configuration is \num{0.21}; most differences this
literature reports are smaller than that. An unpaired comparison at $N=5$ has
almost no power against effects of that size, while pairing removes the seed's
own contribution and leaves the difference under test.

We also report a Wilcoxon signed-rank test, but as a direction check only. Its
smallest attainable two-sided $p$ is $2/2^{N} = \num{0.0625}$ at $N=5$: no
rank-based test on five pairs can clear $\alpha = \num{0.05}$ however large the
effect. Where the two disagree in \S\ref{sec:generality} they are not in
conflict --- the second is at its floor. Reporting it without that caveat would
invite the reader to treat a floor as evidence of absence.

\subsection{Training}

CIFAR-10 at $32\times32$, \num{200} epochs, batch size \num{128}, AdamW with a
cosine schedule and \num{500} warm-up steps: \num{78200} optimiser steps per
run. The recipe is the published one and is held identical across arms; only
the objective changes. Exponential moving averaging is reported as its own
configuration rather than mixed in, because the published runs omit it and
mixing the two would confound the comparison with a second change.

Objectives carrying learnable parameters --- the uncertainty-conditioned
weighting and the GradNorm balancer --- place them in their own optimiser group
with their own learning rate. Without this they stay frozen at initialisation
and the run silently degenerates into a fixed-weight objective, which looks
like a working experiment and tests nothing.

\subsection{Sampling and metrics}

Sampling uses DDIM \citep{song2021denoising} with \num{100} steps and
\num{10000} samples per checkpoint, with the sampling seed fixed across arms so
a comparison differs by the trained model alone.  \S\ref{sec:sampler-check}
re-scores the tier-1 arms under DDPM-1000 to verify the null is not specific
to this operating point. FID is computed with
\texttt{clean-fid} \citep{parmar2022aliased}, which fixes the resizing and
quantisation discrepancies that make FID values incomparable between codebases;
Inception Score and KID come from \texttt{torch-fidelity}. \S\ref{sec:replication}
explains why we do not report a bespoke KID estimator.

Spectra are radial FFT profiles in decibels ($20\log_{10}|F|$), averaged over
images, with both sides resized to a common resolution before profiling. The
resize is not cosmetic: FFT magnitude scales with pixel count, so profiles
taken at different resolutions differ by tens of dB from image size alone. We
report this because a first attempt at the DreamBooth comparison omitted it and
produced a \SI{26}{\decibel} deficit that was almost entirely that offset.

\subsection{What is released}

Tables and figures are generated from the results ledger by scripts in the
repository; no number in this paper is transcribed by hand. The ledger records
one row per evaluated checkpoint with the configuration that produced it, so a
reader can recompute every comparison, including the ones we do not make.
