\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

This paper asks what a measured spectral change is worth in FID at the
operating point of the model that achieves it. Five lines of work bear on the
question, and each subsection below ends with how the present work relates to
its line.

\subsection{Spectral behaviour of generative models}
\label{sec:related-spectral}

Generated images differ systematically from real ones in their Fourier
statistics. For generative adversarial networks (GANs), the high-frequency
discrepancy has been used to detect generated images, traced to the
generator's upsampling operators and removed by architectural or training
changes
\citep{durall2020watch,dzanic2020fourier,frank2020leveraging,schwarz2021frequency,chandrasegaran2021closer,jung2021spectral}.
Diffusion models also underestimate high frequencies.
\citet{ricker2024towards} attribute this gap to the training objective, which
is the premise that frequency-aware objectives act on;
\citet{yang2023diffusion} find that different frequency components are
recovered at different timesteps; and \citet{corvi2023detection} characterise
their forensic traces, including in the Fourier domain.

A second reading attributes the deficit to under-training.
\citet{wang2025spectral} solve the gradient-flow dynamics of linear and
convolutional linear denoisers and find that the time a Fourier mode takes to
reach its target variance scales inversely with that variance, so that
low-variance fine detail is learned last. They confirm the law in U-Nets built
from multilayer perceptrons and report that convolutional U-Nets depart from
it. Our backbone is a convolutional U-Net, so the theory motivates, but does
not settle, this reading. Section~\ref{sec:budget} reports, descriptively,
that over a fourfold budget (with weight averaging enabled) the baseline's FID
improves while its high-band deficit grows.

This literature motivates frequency-aware objectives by an implication that it
seldom tests: if the spectrum is wrong, correcting it should improve the
samples as the metric scores them. The pricing test measures it directly, by
applying the correction to a baseline's own samples.

\subsection{Loss weighting across noise levels}
\label{sec:related-timestep}

Frequency and noise level are coupled in diffusion. Natural-image spectra
decay with frequency whereas isotropic Gaussian noise is flat, so each noise
level acts as a soft low-pass cutoff: high frequencies are submerged first in
the forward process and resolved last in the reverse process
\citep{rissanen2023generative,hoogeboom2023blurring,dieleman2024spectral,falck2025fourier}.
The correspondence has also been used to shape the noise and its schedule
\citep{jiralerspong2025shaping,esteves2026spectrally}. A weighting defined
over frequency therefore acts in part as a weighting over noise levels.
Section~\ref{sec:confound} shows how this coupling enters AWWL, and
Section~\ref{sec:confound-family} that a weighting keyed on the error's
spectrum inherits it.

The parameterisation already fixes an implicit weighting. The $\epsilon$-MSE
is an $x_0$-MSE weighted by the signal-to-noise ratio
\citep{kingma2021variational,salimans2022progressive}, and the residual on
which a frequency-aware objective acts is a scaled clean-image error, with
image structure, rather than white noise (Eq.~\eqref{eq:residual}). Explicit
schemes act on this axis: \citet{nichol2021improved} importance-sample
timesteps by loss magnitude, P2 weighting \citep{choi2022perception}
emphasises the noise levels at which perceptually rich content forms, and
Min-SNR\nobreakdash-$\gamma$ \citep{hang2023efficient} weights the $x_0$-error of each
timestep by $\min(\mathrm{SNR}_t, \gamma)$. EDM \citep{karras2022elucidating}
separates the loss weighting from the noise-level distribution and the
preconditioning, EDM2 \citep{karras2024analyzing} learns the weighting in the
manner of multi-task uncertainty weighting \citep{kendall2018multi}, and the
appropriate schedule shifts towards higher noise as resolution grows
\citep{chen2023importance,hoogeboom2023simple}. On the theoretical side, a
weighting that does not increase with log-SNR makes the weighted loss the
evidence lower bound (ELBO) of Gaussian-noise-augmented data
\citep{kingma2023understanding}. \citet{kumar2025loss} compare such objectives
empirically, and the Soft-Min-SNR weight of \citet{li2026smoothed} matches
Min-SNR-$\gamma$ at leading order, which makes Min-SNR-$\gamma$ a principled
reference curve.

The total weight of a sub-band weighting is a function of the noise level, and
hence a weighting of the kind \citet{kingma2023understanding} analyse. For
AWWL \citep{thao2026awwl} it is anti-correlated with Min-SNR-$\gamma$ at the
CIFAR-10 setting ($r = \rSMinSNRCifar$) and correlated with it at the
DreamBooth setting ($r = +\rSMinSNRDB$; Section~\ref{sec:confound}). The
redistribution across bands at a fixed noise level is not covered by that
account; its closest principled counterpart is the frequency-dependent forward
process of \citet{hoogeboom2023blurring}, which changes the process rather
than the loss. The multi-task view also supplies alternatives to hand-designed
band weights, uncertainty weighting \citep{kendall2018multi} and GradNorm
\citep{chen2018gradnorm}, which Section~\ref{sec:extensions} evaluates across
sub-bands. Its uncertainty-weighted arm extends the EDM2 weighting to one
weight per band and so learns a noise-level schedule and a band split
together.

\subsection{Frequency-aware objectives and architectures}
\label{sec:related-objectives}

Several objectives reweight the loss across frequency. Focal Frequency Loss
(FFL) \citep{jiang2021focal}, a term added to spatial losses for
reconstruction and synthesis, weights each Fourier component by the magnitude
of its current error, normalised per sample, and has no noise-level schedule.
\citet{yang2023diffusion} weight a distillation loss inversely to spectrum
magnitude to promote high-frequency recovery, and AWWL weights the one-level
wavelet sub-bands of the $\epsilon$-residual on a schedule tied to $\sigma_t$.
Section~\ref{sec:second-member} trains FFL alone on the $\epsilon$-residual,
in place of the MSE, as a second member of the family: by keying the weight on
the error rather than on a schedule, it departs furthest from AWWL while
keeping its premise.

The closest contemporary relative of AWWL is the work of
\citet{chandran2026spectral}, who add Fourier and wavelet losses to the
diffusion objective while briefly fine-tuning pretrained EDM checkpoints. On
class-conditional CIFAR-10 they find every variant within the standard
deviation of the EDM baseline, in agreement with Section~\ref{sec:results},
where AWWL shows no gain on unconditional CIFAR-10. On AFHQv2 and
FFHQ at $64\times64$ they report reductions of \numrange{0.02}{0.07}~FID on
baselines of \numrange{2.04}{2.56} over three seeds, whereas the change
published for AWWL was $\pubDFIDAWWL$~FID on a baseline of \pubFIDMSE{}, from
a single run \citep{thao2026awwl}. Effects of this size must first be settled
against seed variance (Section~\ref{sec:protocol}); at $64\times64$,
Section~\ref{sec:celeba} computes the value side of the pricing test for a
baseline, but AWWL was not trained at that resolution
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

Methods that change the representation or the process rather than the loss,
such as wavelet-domain diffusion for sampling speed \citep{phung2023wavelet}
or restoration \citep{huang2024wavedm} and wavelet score-based models that
factorise the distribution across scales \citep{guth2022wavelet}, lie outside
the audited family. Their stated aims are efficiency or conditional
restoration rather than a named change to a statistic of the output, so the
pricing test does not apply to them without a further specification of that
statistic.

\subsection{Post-hoc spectral correction and metric sensitivity}
\label{sec:related-metrics}

Spectral operations on images are established as methods. Post-processing has
been used to make the spectra of generated images mimic real ones and so evade
spectrum-based detectors \citep{dzanic2020fourier,dong2022think}. At inference
time, FreeU \citep{si2024freeu} rebalances backbone and skip-connection
features inside the U-Net, \citet{yu2025frequency} regulate wavelet sub-band
energy during sampling to mitigate exposure bias, and \citet{oh2026attention}
modulate cross-attention in the Fourier domain. The pricing test uses the same
kind of operation as a measurement: it applies to the baseline's finished
samples the change that the trained objective achieves, and reads the result
as the value of that change.

Whether such a change registers depends on the metric. FID moves substantially
under resizing and compression \citep{parmar2022aliased}, so it registers
low-level changes of the kind a spectral operator makes. It is also biased at
finite sample size by a model-dependent amount \citep{chong2020effectively},
and it is strongly affected by ImageNet class information and can be reduced
substantially without any gain in perceived quality
\citep{kynkaanniemi2023role}. \citet{stein2023exposing} find that the standard
metrics rank diffusion models against GANs in ways human raters do not
reproduce, and recommend DINOv2 features in place of Inception-V3.
\citet{jayasumana2024rethinking} propose the CLIP-based maximum mean
discrepancy (CMMD), and \citet{barratt2018note} catalogue the failure modes of
the Inception Score. \citet{heusel2017gans} calibrated FID by applying large
disturbances to real images, starting from the metric's minimum.

Two consequences follow for pricing. First, FID can move without a change in
perceived quality, so the value of a change is its worth in the metric, not a
demonstrated gain in quality. The pricing test inherits the metric's validity
rather than establishing it, and one of its preconditions is that the metric
registers the statistic at all (Section~\ref{sec:pricing-pre}). Second, a
calibration at the metric's minimum measures a different quantity from the
value at a trained model's operating point. On held-out CIFAR-10 test images,
whose FID against the training set is \realTestFID{}, white noise of $\pm0.5$~LSB
does not lower FID (value $\realNoiseHalfValue$) and noise of $\pm1$~LSB
raises it ($\realNoiseOneValue$), in single measurements. Applied to the baseline's
samples, $\pm0.5$~LSB is worth $+\priceNoiseHalfValue$
(Section~\ref{sec:worth}). The pricing test therefore measures the value at
the operating point (Section~\ref{sec:pricing-def}).

\subsection{Attribution of empirical gains}
\label{sec:related-attribution}

Failure to identify the sources of empirical gains is a recurring weakness of
machine-learning scholarship \citep{lipton2019troubling}. Controlled
re-evaluations often find claimed improvements smaller than reported or absent
\citep{melis2018state,lucic2018gans,musgrave2020metric}, and benchmark
conclusions frequently do not survive accounting for sources of variance,
seeds among them \citep{bouthillier2021accounting}. For FID,
\citet{dufour2026fid} find across several hundred networks that retraining
with a different seed moves FID $3.2\times$ more than redrawing samples, and
recommend treating gaps below roughly \SI{1.3}{\percent} as inconclusive.
This, with guidance on power, interval estimation and significance testing at
small seed counts \citep{colas2018many,agarwal2021deep,dror2018hitchhiker},
informs the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:protocol}.

In several areas a training-time intervention is expected to outperform a
post-hoc counterpart that targets the same quantity. Examples are temperature
scaling for calibration \citep{guo2017calibration}, post-hoc logit adjustment
for long-tailed recognition, which \citet{menon2021long} present alongside its
loss-based equivalent, and post-processing of a trained predictor to meet a
fairness criterion \citep{hardt2016equality}. Closer to the present setting,
\citet{kynkaanniemi2023role} change a statistic of a generated set after the
fact, by resampling it to match class histograms, and read the response of
FID. The accounting identity of the pricing test is an analogue of the
separation of direct from indirect effects in causal mediation analysis
\citep{robins1992identifiability,pearl2001direct};
Section~\ref{sec:pricing-reading} states the correspondence and its
assumptions.

Relative to each of these lines the pricing test is narrower and more
specific. It prices a mechanism that the objective itself states, in the
reported metric, at the model's operating point, using the baseline's own
samples. It does not establish that the metric is valid, and it applies only
to objectives that name the statistic they intend to change. To our knowledge,
no prior work prices an objective's stated output statistic by applying an
operator to the baseline's own samples.
