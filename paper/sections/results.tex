\section{Auditing AWWL}
\label{sec:audit}
\label{sec:results}

This section audits AWWL on CIFAR-10. Unless stated otherwise, runs are
evaluated at epoch~199 with DDIM-100 and \num{10000} samples, and
differences from MSE are paired by seed with 95\% $t$-intervals; supporting
material is in Appendix~\ref{app:supp}.

\subsection{Replication of the published results}
\label{sec:replication}

\input{tables/replication}

The baseline reproduces; the wavelet objectives do not. The conference paper
\citep{thao2026awwl} reports one run per objective, sampled with ancestral
DDPM-1000 and without EMA, in which AWWL has the highest Inception Score and
an FID level with the baseline's, and the static wavelet arm has the lowest
FID. Table~\ref{tab:replication} sets these FID values beside five-seed
replications sampled with DDPM-1000, as published, and with DDIM-100
(Inception Score in Table~\ref{tab:replication-details}). Under DDPM-1000
the replicated MSE mean lies within one seed standard deviation of the
published value ($\pubOffsetSDMSE$~SD), whereas those of the static wavelet
arm and AWWL lie \pubOffsetSDStatic{} and \pubOffsetSDAWWL~SD above
theirs, and paired by seed both arms are significantly worse than MSE. The
published FID differences lie outside the 95\% prediction interval for one
further paired run ($\pubDFIDAWWL$ against
$[\piFIDAWWLDdpmLo, \piFIDAWWLDdpmHi]$ for AWWL), so seed variation cannot
account for them, though it can for AWWL's published Inception Score gain,
which lies inside its interval (Appendix~\ref{app:supp-replication}). No Inception Score difference is
significant under either sampler, so neither wavelet objective improves
significantly on MSE on either metric.

The objective audited here is the released implementation. The conference
paper writes the detail term as a sum over the three detail bands (its Eq.~7;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:summed} here), whereas the released code that produced its
results averages them (Eq.~\eqref{eq:audited}), the form used for every AWWL
result in this paper. Trained as written, AWWL with detail bands summed is
worse than MSE by $+\dEqSeven$~FID (Holm $p = \dEqSevenHolm$;
Table~\ref{tab:compare-fid}), and although it triples the ratio of detail to
approximation weight, its high-band change is smaller than that of the
averaged form ($+\corrHighEqSeven$ against $+\corrHighAWWL$~dB;
Table~\ref{tab:bands}). A reimplementation from the published equation
therefore yields a different, worse objective.

No objective in the matrix of \nArmsMatrix{} configurations (MSE, the
\nFamilyMain{} arms of the main family, AWWL + EMA and the timestep-only arm)
and \nRunsMatrix{} runs has a significantly lower FID than MSE
(Fig.~\ref{fig:effects}), and only MSE + EMA has a negative point estimate
($\dMSEema$ $[\dMSEemaLo, \dMSEemaHi]$, three seeds). On the other four
metrics (Table~\ref{tab:compare-other}) the only differences in an
objective's favour that survive correction are gains in precision, by
Min-SNR-$\gamma$ and the learned weighting, and both lose recall
significantly.

AWWL's FID exceeds that of MSE by $+\dAWWL$ $[\dAWWLLo, \dAWWLHi]$ (raw
$p = \dAWWLP$; Holm $p = \dAWWLHolm$ over the \nFamilyMain{} arms of the main
family). Read as a bound, the interval excludes any mean difference below
$\dAWWLLo$~FID, so an improvement larger than that is ruled out; it does not
exclude a harm of moderate size, since at five seeds the test detects a true
difference of \mdeAWWL~FID with 80\% power (Section~\ref{sec:setup-stats}). No
other metric favours AWWL: Inception Score, KID and precision do not differ
from those of MSE even before correction, and recall is lower at nominal
significance only (raw $p = \dRecAWWLP$, Holm $p = \dRecAWWLHolm$). Against
MSE + EMA, AWWL + EMA is behind by $+\dEMApair$~FID ($p = \dEMApairP$);
three seeds give no sign that weight averaging alters the comparison, though
they cannot exclude it. The intermediate checkpoints of the same runs (epochs 39 to 159;
Appendix~\ref{app:supp-convergence}) give no evidence that AWWL reaches a
given quality with less training: no difference favours it significantly.

Of the \nFamilyMain{} arms of the main family, all except Huber, the static
wavelet arm, AWWL and MSE + EMA are significantly worse than MSE after Holm
correction, among them $L_1$, Charbonnier, Min-SNR-$\gamma$, the family's
second member, Focal Frequency Loss (FFL; Section~\ref{sec:second-member}),
the four normalisation controls (Section~\ref{sec:confound}), AWWL with
detail bands summed and every extension (Section~\ref{sec:extensions}). Three
arms did not train stably; their rows record a failed optimisation, not an
effect size: raw SNR weighting, whose weight is unbounded as
$t \to 0$ (Section~\ref{sec:notation}), GradNorm balancing and the
three-level decomposition (Appendix~\ref{app:supp-extensions}). Outside the
main family, the timestep-only arm (Section~\ref{sec:confound}) is also worse
($+\dTimestep$~FID, Holm $p = \dTimestepHolm$ in its own two-test family).

\subsection{Sensitivity to the sampler}
\label{sec:sampler}

The sampler reverses no comparison and widens AWWL's gap. The conference
paper sampled with DDPM-1000 and the matrix uses DDIM-100 to keep
\nRunsMatrix{} evaluations affordable, so MSE, the static wavelet arm and AWWL
were also sampled with DDPM-1000 from the same weights and sampling seed
(Table~\ref{tab:replication}; KID in Table~\ref{tab:sampler}). FID falls for
every arm, least for AWWL (by \dropAWWL{} against \dropMSE{} for MSE), so
AWWL's gap grows from $+\dAWWL$ to $+\ddpmDAWWL$ and the static wavelet arm,
not significantly different from MSE under DDIM-100, becomes significantly
worse (Section~\ref{sec:replication}); KID agrees for AWWL (Holm
$p = \ddpmDKIDAWWLHolm$), and Inception Score separates neither arm. Under
DDPM-1000 AWWL's high-band change also nearly vanishes and changes sign
($\corrHighAWWLddpm$ against $+\corrHighAWWL$~dB;
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}). DDIM-100 is therefore the more favourable of the
two operating points to AWWL, and the primary matrix does not understate its
effect.

\subsection{Four times the training budget}
\label{sec:budget}

A budget four times as long gives AWWL no advantage over MSE, although three
seeds cannot resolve the gap. The 200-epoch recipe has about a tenth of the
optimiser steps of the original DDPM recipe \citep{ho2020denoising}, and the high-frequency deficit
of diffusion samples has been read as a symptom of under-training
(Section~\ref{sec:related-spectral}); an objective that targets the deficit
might then pay only near convergence. MSE and AWWL were retrained for 800
epochs (\num{312800} steps; three seeds each, EMA with decay \num{0.9999}, the
cosine schedule stretched to the run; Appendix~\ref{app:setup-budget}), which
lowers the FID of MSE with EMA from \budFIDMSEShortEmaDdim{} to
\budFIDMSELongDdim{} (DDIM-100). AWWL's point estimates remain behind MSE
under both samplers, but neither gap is significant even before correction
(Table~\ref{tab:budget}), and the DDIM-100 test has only \budPowerLong\%
power against a true gap of 0.3~FID; Inception Score and KID do not separate
the arms either. These data show no advantage for AWWL but do not establish
that its disadvantage persists. Nor does longer training close the deficit:
while FID improves, MSE's high-band deficit grows, end against end, by
\trajEndToEnd~dB (Table~\ref{tab:deficit-budget}).

The step count alone does not set the operating point. At the same
\num{78200} steps, the epoch-199 checkpoints of the 800-epoch runs carry EMA
and are taken at about 85\% of the peak learning rate, whereas the matrix's
main arms have no EMA and a rate annealed to zero. Even against the matrix's
MSE + EMA, the MSE checkpoint has a lower FID and a smaller high-band deficit
(\trajDefMSEOneNinetyNine{} against \trajDefMSEend{}~dB), and its FID is
lowest at epoch~\longBestEpochMSE{}, rising as the rate decays
(Table~\ref{tab:deficit-budget}). The comparison is descriptive
(Appendix~\ref{app:setup-budget}), but the matrix's FID level and the deficit
AWWL targets depend on where the schedule ends, not only on its length.

\subsection{Extensions of the weighting}
\label{sec:extensions}

None of the seven arms that implement the extensions proposed in the
conference paper improves on MSE (Fig.~\ref{fig:effects}) or on the parent
with which each is compared. Each was trained at one untuned configuration
(Appendix~\ref{app:setup-arms}) to test whether AWWL's shortfall is specific
to its two-parameter schedule. Against AWWL with detail bands summed, the
parent of the per-sub-band, spatially adaptive and learned-basis arms, and
with Holm correction over the seven extensions
(Table~\ref{tab:extensions}), the per-sub-band schedules have no detectable
effect (Holm $p = \dSubbandVsParentHolm$), whereas spatial adaptation and the
learned basis cost a further $+\dSpatialVsParent$ and $+\dLiftingVsParent$~FID
(Holm $p = \dSpatialVsParentHolm$ and $\dLiftingVsParentHolm$). These two
arms and the all-axes arm cannot test their axis as built (the spatial map
is computed from the noise target, and the lifting filters are trained on
the loss they define; Appendix~\ref{app:setup-arms}), so their costs
describe these implementations only. For every priced extension the value of the spectral change is small beside the FID gap to
MSE, which is therefore almost entirely MRG (Table~\ref{tab:pricing-arms}).
The learned weighting, an uncertainty weighting \citep{kendall2018multi}
conditioned on $\sigma_t$ with its total left free, becomes a steep timestep
schedule: each band weight is driven towards the inverse of that band's loss,
which for $\epsilon$-prediction is smallest at high noise, and in every
seed the summed weight at $t = 1000$ is \num{\learnedSumRatioMin} to
\num{\learnedSumRatioMax} times that at $t = 1$,
with the HH weight there up to \learnedHHLLMax{} times the LL weight. This is
the timestep confound of Section~\ref{sec:confound} in extreme form, and the
arm is $+\dLearned$~FID worse than MSE (Holm $p \dLearnedHolm$). Seven
untuned configurations do not show that no band weighting could help.
