\section{Does AWWL change the spectrum?}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Under the DDIM-100 sampler of the main matrix, AWWL changes the statistic $s$
it names (Eq.~\eqref{eq:statistic}), so precondition P1
(Section~\ref{sec:pricing-pre}) holds. A band's deficit is the reference
level minus the samples'; an arm's change is its level minus that of the MSE
run of the same seed (paired mean over five seeds, with its 95\%
$t$-interval). MSE's deficit grows with frequency: \deficitLowMSE,
\deficitMidMSE{} and \deficitHighMSE~dB in the low, mid and high thirds
(Table~\ref{tab:bands}, Fig.~\ref{fig:spectrum}), so the premise of AWWL
holds. Estimated from the first \num{2000} files of each directory instead
(hazard H2, Section~\ref{sec:setup-metrics}), the high-band deficit is
\deficitHighMSEsubset~dB.

\paragraph{AWWL's change.}
AWWL reduces the deficit in every band, by \corrLowAWWL~dB
$[\corrLowAWWLLo, \corrLowAWWLHi]$, \corrMidAWWL~dB
$[\corrMidAWWLLo, \corrMidAWWLHi]$ and \corrHighAWWL~dB
$[\corrHighAWWLLo, \corrHighAWWLHi]$ in the low, mid and high bands, or
\shareLowAWWL, \shareMidAWWL{} and \shareHighAWWL\% of the respective
deficit. A broadband change is consistent with the design: the one-level Haar
detail bands that AWWL up-weights at low noise nominally begin at radius 8,
inside the mid third. The band means weight the radial bins equally,
so the corner bins at radii 19--22, beyond the axial Nyquist radius of 16,
take half the weight of the high-band mean although they hold only \nCoefCorner{} of
its \nCoefHigh{} coefficients. Weighted by coefficient count, AWWL's
high-band change is \corrHighWAWWL~dB $[\corrHighWAWWLLo, \corrHighWAWWLHi]$.

\paragraph{Band shape or timestep weight.}
The normalisation controls of Section~\ref{sec:confound}, which remove the
residual-independent timestep weight $S(\sigma)$ or fix
$\sum_b \omega_b^2$, keep most of the mid- and high-band change
(coefficient-weighted high band: \corrHighWSumFixed~dB
$[\corrHighWSumFixedLo, \corrHighWSumFixedHi]$ with
$\sum_b \omega_b = \levelSumFixed$, the constant-$S$ arm;
Table~\ref{tab:confound}), whereas the timestep-only arm, the pixel MSE
weighted by $S(\sigma)$, moves this statistic by only
\corrHighWTimestep~dB $[\corrHighWTimestepLo, \corrHighWTimestepHi]$ against
AWWL's \corrHighWAWWL~dB. AWWL's spectral change thus comes mainly from its
$\sigma$-dependent band shape, which itself reweights timesteps through the
residual (Proposition~\ref{prop:confound}(b)), so neither comparison
isolates a frequency effect.
Min-SNR-$\gamma$, a timestep reweighting without a frequency term, raises the
unweighted high-band mean by a similar amount, \corrHighMinSNR~dB, but only
through the corner bins. Weighted by coefficient count, its high-band change
is \corrHighWMinSNR~dB $[\corrHighWMinSNRLo, \corrHighWMinSNRHi]$, and at
radii 15--17 it is $\corrInnerHighMinSNR$~dB
$[\corrInnerHighMinSNRLo, \corrInnerHighMinSNRHi]$, against
\corrInnerHighAWWL~dB for AWWL. It leaves the mid band at MSE's level, and
its change is worth $\valueArmMinSNR$~FID
$[\valueArmMinSNRLo, \valueArmMinSNRHi]$, a small loss
(Section~\ref{sec:worth-arms}). A band statistic alone thus does not identify the
mechanism; the pricing test values the whole profile.

\paragraph{Sampler.}
Under DDPM-1000, MSE's high-band deficit is \deficitHighMSEddpm{} instead of
\deficitHighMSE~dB, a larger shift than any objective in Table~\ref{tab:bands}
produces except FFL (Section~\ref{sec:second-member}), and AWWL does not
reduce it ($\corrHighAWWLddpm$~dB; Table~\ref{tab:bands-ddpm}). P1 therefore
holds under DDIM-100 only; under DDPM-1000, with almost no change to
price, the MRG would be about AWWL's whole FID gap, $+\ddpmDAWWL$
(Section~\ref{sec:sampler}).
