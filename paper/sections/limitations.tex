\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

A paper reporting that a method does not work owes the reader a clear account
of the conditions under which it was tested, since every one of them is a way
the result could fail to generalise.

\paragraph{The pricing argument rests on two tasks, the rest on more.}
The replication and the confound are measured on CIFAR-10 at $32\times32$ with
a pixel-space U-Net and on DreamBooth at $512\times512$ with a latent-space
U-Net --- two architectures, two resolutions, two tasks. The \emph{pricing}
argument of \S\ref{sec:worth}, which is this paper's central move, is measured
on both, but its FID form only on the first. Absence of an effect at
$32\times32$ does not establish absence at resolutions where high-frequency
content carries more of the signal Inception features encode. We expect the
trade a frequency-aware objective makes to look different there, and we have
not measured it.

\paragraph{One objective.}
We audit a single published objective in detail, together with its own
method-level extensions (\S\ref{sec:extensions}); the family's other
published members we do not run. The title's claim is about a
family, and one member is not a family. What transfers beyond this instance is
argued rather than measured: Eq.~\ref{eq:parseval} constrains any sub-band
weighting on an orthonormal basis, the confound of \S\ref{sec:confound} arises
whenever a weighting depends on $\sigma_t$ without its total held fixed, and
the post-processing control applies to any method that names the statistic it
intends to change. Whether other members clear those bars is an empirical
question we have not answered.

\paragraph{Sampling is DDIM-100 throughout.}
The comparison is internally consistent and the sampling seed is fixed across
arms, but it is one operating point. Few-step samplers produce smoother output,
which is precisely where a frequency-aware objective should have its advantage,
so the choice is not neutral with respect to the hypothesis.

\paragraph{The post-process is not the model's own failure mode.}
The Fourier correction of \S\ref{sec:worth} raises a band; a model
under-produces that band through its architecture and objective, and the two
leave different spatial signatures. The value the post-process assigns to the
correction is therefore an estimate of what the spectral change is worth, not
an exact accounting of what the objective did. We take the direction of the
result to be robust and the magnitude to carry that caveat.

\paragraph{Our own implementations are not authoritative.}
The spectral distance is a hand-written radial FFT. Two independent
implementations here agree, which is reassuring and is not proof --- and after
\S\ref{sec:replication}, where a hand-written KID estimator inverted the sign
of the study's only positive result, we are in no position to treat agreement
between our own tools as settling anything. Where a standard implementation
exists we use it and say so.

\paragraph{The pricing test needs a metric that can see the statistic.}
\S\ref{sec:generality} prices a near-complete spectral correction on DreamBooth
at zero: removing the deficit entirely moves CLIP score and subject similarity
by two orders of magnitude less than the seed variance. That is a real result
about the task, but it also exposes a precondition. A price of zero has two
readings --- the change does not matter, or the metric cannot register it ---
and the test does not separate them on its own. Reporting the residual deficit
alongside the price is what makes the second reading checkable, and anyone
applying the test elsewhere should report both.

\paragraph{Three things change at once between our two tasks.}
Moving from CIFAR-10 to DreamBooth changes the architecture, the resolution and
the task simultaneously. That is enough to show no conclusion rests on a single
training setup, and not enough to attribute any difference between the two to
any one of the three. Separating them needs a third setting that varies one
axis alone, which we have not run.

\paragraph{Metric scope.}
FID, IS and KID are Inception-feature statistics and inherit that network's
priorities \citep{kynkaanniemi2023role, stein2023exposing}. An objective could
improve images in ways these metrics do not register. Our claim is bounded
accordingly: the objective does not improve the metrics the literature it
belongs to reports, and the spectral change it makes is not worth enough in
those metrics to cover what it costs.
