\section{What the spectral change is worth}
\label{sec:worth}

This section applies the pricing test (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pricing}) with $B$
the MSE arm and $A$ AWWL at epoch 199 of the main recipe, sampled with
DDIM-100, under which AWWL changes the spectrum (P1;
Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}); operators act on $B$'s \num{10000} samples in
each of five seeds. $M$ is FID; brackets give 95\% $t$-intervals of paired
differences, and $p$ is the two-sided paired $t$-test, raw unless marked Holm.

\subsection{The price curve and measurement hazard H1}
\label{sec:worth-curve}

FID registers the spectral statistic on CIFAR-10, so precondition P4 holds.
The value of the unquantised raised-cosine boost rises monotonically
with the high-band change it produces
(Fig.~\ref{fig:price-curve}(a); Table~\ref{tab:price-curve}), by
\priceBoostPerDB~FID per decibel up to the 0.8~dB dose. Because the boost's
ramp also lifts the upper mid-band bins (Section~\ref{sec:pricing-ops}), the
high band is priced alone by band-restricted operators
(Fig.~\ref{fig:price-curve}(b)), and FID registers it: the high-band match is
worth $\priceMatchHighValue$ $[\priceMatchHighValueLo, \priceMatchHighValueHi]$.

\paragraph{Measurement hazard H1: re-quantisation.}
Re-quantising filtered images to 8 bits changes the price by more than the
filter does. The 0.41~dB boost, the dose that reproduces AWWL's high-band
change, is worth $\priceBoostDitherValue$ when rounded with uniform dither and
$\priceBoostValue$ in floating point (Table~\ref{tab:operators}). Uniform
white noise of $\pm0.5$ and $\pm1$~LSB per channel, added without filtering
or rounding, improves FID by $\priceNoiseHalfValue$ and $\priceNoiseOneValue$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:price-curve}(a)), far above the boost's curve at the high-band changes the noise produces, so an operator re-quantised with
dither inherits a gain out of all proportion to its change in $s$. Every
value below is therefore scored in floating point (P0); the trained arms are
all quantised once, identically, so H1 does not bias comparisons between
them. The noise gain is a property of the operating point, not of FID in
general: on the \num{10000} held-out CIFAR-10 test images (FID
\realTestFID{} against the training set), noise of $\pm0.5$~LSB does not
lower FID (value $\realNoiseHalfValue$) and noise of $\pm1$~LSB raises it
($\realNoiseOneValue$; single measurements). The real-image control was not
repeated on CelebA-64, where the noise also lowers the FID of the baseline's
samples (Section~\ref{sec:celeba}).

\subsection{What AWWL's change is worth, and what it costs}
\label{sec:worth-headline}

AWWL's spectral change is worth $V = \priceTransplantValue$~FID
$[\priceTransplantValueLo, \priceTransplantValueHi]$, and AWWL falls short of
the baseline carrying that change by $\mathrm{MRG} = \mrgMain$
$[\mrgMainLo, \mrgMainHi]$ (Table~\ref{tab:accounting}; raw $p = \mrgMainP$).
Its Holm-adjusted $p$ over the \nFamilyPricing{} arms of the pricing family
(Section~\ref{sec:worth-arms}) is \mrgArmAWWLHolm, so the MRG does not
survive the family-wise correction. The family includes both EMA arms, as
declared; over the \nFamilyPricingObjectives{} arms that are training objectives, without
the two weight-averaging rows, the corrected $p$ is \mrgArmAWWLHolmNoEMA, so the
family-wise verdict is borderline and depends on the family.
The transplant is the primary operator because it imposes AWWL's change in
every radial bin, seed by seed (P2); it replaced, after a first analysis,
the 0.41~dB boost re-quantised to 8 bits, whose value H1 inflates.
Unquantised, the boost gives $\mathrm{MRG} = \mrgBoost$ $[\mrgBoostLo, \mrgBoostHi]$
($p = \mrgBoostP$), smaller as it credits less of the change.

By Eq.~\eqref{eq:accounting}, which holds seed by seed,
$D = M(A) - M(B) = \dAWWL = \mrgMain - \priceTransplantValue$. A positive MRG
thus says that what AWWL changes besides the spectrum costs FID on balance,
not that this cost exceeds what the spectral change earns; the latter is the
statement $D > 0$, which five seeds do not resolve ($[\dAWWLLo, \dAWWLHi]$,
$p = \dAWWLP$). In KID ($\times 10^{3}$; the \texttt{clean-fid}
implementation of the scoring path, Appendix~\ref{app:scoring}, not the
\texttt{torch-fidelity} one of Table~\ref{tab:compare-other}) the value
replicates ($\kidTransplantValue$ $[\kidTransplantValueLo, \kidTransplantValueHi]$), but
the MRG, of the same sign, is not resolved ($\kidMrgMain$
$[\kidMrgMainLo, \kidMrgMainHi]$, $p = \kidMrgMainP$), since AWWL's KID gap to
MSE is only $\kidGapAWWL$ there. On the 800-epoch schedule (three seeds;
Section~\ref{sec:budget}) the MRG is positive but unresolved both at MSE's
FID optimum, epoch~\longBestEpochMSE{} ($\mrgLongOpt$
$[\mrgLongOptLo, \mrgLongOptHi]$, not tabulated), and at epoch 799
($\mrgLong$ $[\mrgLongLo, \mrgLongHi]$; Table~\ref{tab:accounting}).

\paragraph{Value by band.}
FID rewards chiefly the mid-band part of AWWL's change, and little of the
high-band part that motivates the objective. Restricted to one third of the radial bins
(Fig.~\ref{fig:price-curve}(b)), the transplant is worth $V_{\mathrm{low}} = \priceTransplantLowValue$
$[\priceTransplantLowValueLo, \priceTransplantLowValueHi]$,
$V_{\mathrm{mid}} = \priceTransplantMidValue$
$[\priceTransplantMidValueLo, \priceTransplantMidValueHi]$ and
$V_{\mathrm{high}} = \priceTransplantHighValue$
$[\priceTransplantHighValueLo, \priceTransplantHighValueHi]$. These values
approximately add (sum \priceTransplantBandSum, against
\priceTransplantValue), so single-band attribution is meaningful for this
change. The value thus sits in radii \bandMidMin--\bandMidMax, the lower part
of the range AWWL up-weights (its one-level Haar detail bands begin at
radius 8; Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}), not in radii
\bandHighMin--\bandHighMax, mostly corner frequencies beyond the axial
Nyquist radius. The attribution is a property of FID as implemented:
\texttt{clean-fid} upsamples each $32\times32$ sample bicubically to
$299\times299$ pixels before the Inception network
(Appendix~\ref{app:scoring}), which attenuates the outer radii more than the
mid band, so the split, like $V$ itself, is specific to the metric.

\paragraph{The match as a scale.}
\label{sec:second-operator}
The full radial match, which removes the mean deficit in every bin, is worth
$\priceMatchValue$ $[\priceMatchValueLo, \priceMatchValueHi]$, and AWWL's
change is worth \priceTransplantShareOfMatch\% as much. The match is a scale,
not a ceiling: the mid-band match alone is worth more
($\priceMatchMidValue$ $[\priceMatchMidValueLo, \priceMatchMidValueHi]$), so
FID is not monotone in the distance to the reference spectrum. AWWL's gap to
the matched baseline, \mrgMatch~FID, is not a cost: the match applies far more correction than
AWWL achieves, violating P2.

\subsection{Checks on the reading}
\label{sec:worth-checks}

\paragraph{Operator dependence (P3).}
$V$ depends on the operator: the three operators are coherent radial gains,
whereas FID values incoherent energy (white noise) far more per decibel
(Fig.~\ref{fig:price-curve}(a)), and similar high-band changes are priced
differently (Section~\ref{sec:worth-arms}). The transplant is
canonical only in reproducing $\delta_A$ in every bin
(Section~\ref{sec:pricing-ops}); a training-made change may be priced
otherwise.

\paragraph{No interaction (P5).}
Removing AWWL's change from its own samples costs
$V_A = \priceReverseValue$~FID, against $V = \priceTransplantValue$ for adding
it to MSE's; the paired difference, \interactionTransplant{}
$[\interactionTransplantLo, \interactionTransplantHi]$
($p = \interactionTransplantP$), is small and unresolved, consistent with P5,
as is that of the boost ($\priceBoostDiffAWWL$,
$p = \priceBoostDiffAWWLP$; Table~\ref{tab:worth}).

\paragraph{Controls.}
The positive control prices the gain of longer training, which targets
nothing spectral but also lowers the learning rate from about 85\% to 50\% of
its peak: the three 800-epoch MSE runs (with EMA)
at epoch~\longBestEpochMSE{}, their FID optimum ($A$), against their
epoch-199 checkpoints ($B$). Training longer improves FID by
$\posOptTrainGain$, the transplanted change is worth $\posOptValue$
(unresolved), and the MRG is significantly negative,
$\posOptMRG$ $[\posOptMRGLo, \posOptMRGHi]$ ($p = \posOptMRGP$), as it is
with $A$ at epoch 799 ($\posCtrlMRG$, $p = \posCtrlMRGP$). The
null control, the static wavelet arm, moves the high band by only
$\corrHighStatic$~dB, worth $V = \valueArmStatic$, and returns
$\mathrm{MRG} = \mrgArmStatic$ $[\mrgArmStaticLo, \mrgArmStaticHi]$ (Holm
$p = \mrgArmStaticHolm$). As $|V|$ is small in both, $\mathrm{MRG} \approx
D$: the controls do not test assumption~(ii) of
Section~\ref{sec:pricing-reading}.

\paragraph{A planted mechanism.}
\label{sec:worth-planted}
The assumption can be tested for AWWL's change by delivering that change
through training alone. We trained MSE, five seeds, on CIFAR-10 images that
each carry AWWL's measured change, imposed by the per-radius gain of the
transplant (the seed mean of $s(A) - s(B)$, applied in floating point;
Appendix~\ref{app:setup}). Training reproduced the planted change: the arm's
samples differ from MSE's by \corrPlantedLow, \corrPlantedMid{} and
\corrPlantedHigh~dB in the low, mid and high thirds, against AWWL's
\corrLowAWWL, \corrMidAWWL{} and \corrHighAWWL. Its gap to MSE is
$D = \dPlanted$ $[\dPlantedLo, \dPlantedHi]$~FID ($p = \dPlantedP$), the
transplant prices its change at $V = \valuePlanted$
$[\valuePlantedLo, \valuePlantedHi]$, and its MRG is $\mrgPlanted$
$[\mrgPlantedLo, +\mrgPlantedHi]$ ($p = \mrgPlantedP$), and $\fddMrgPlanted$
$[\fddMrgPlantedLo, +\fddMrgPlantedHi]$ in DINOv2 features
(Section~\ref{sec:worth-dino}). A spectral change made by training is thus
valued as the filter values it, to within about $0.25$~FID, where AWWL's MRG
is \mrgMain: for this change the identifying assumption holds, and the MRG
separates a gain delivered by the mechanism (the planted arm) from a cost
incurred outside it (AWWL).

\paragraph{Sensitivity to the identifying assumption.}
For changes that are not planted, we ask how far the assumption may fail. If the
training-made change were worth $V_{\mathrm{train}}$, the cost outside the
change, $D + V_{\mathrm{train}}$, would keep a positive uncorrected 95\%
interval as long as $V_{\mathrm{train}}$ exceeded the tipping point
$V^\ast = \tipValue$~FID, \tipShare\% of the filter's value
\priceTransplantValue. Because $V^\ast$ is minus the lower bound of the
uncorrected interval of $D$, it bounds the raw verdict only: after Holm
correction the MRG is unresolved even at $V_{\mathrm{train}} = V$
(Holm $p = \mrgArmAWWLHolm$).

\subsection{The test across arms}
\label{sec:worth-arms}

The pricing family holds \nFamilyPricing{} arms, each priced against MSE
without EMA: the stably trained arms of the main family (all but raw SNR
weighting, GradNorm balancing and the three-level arm) and AWWL + EMA
(Fig.~\ref{fig:pricing-arms}; Table~\ref{tab:pricing-arms}). For each,
$\mathrm{MRG} = D + V$ holds exactly. No frequency-aware arm has a negative
MRG estimate, and the MRG is Holm-significant for all of them except AWWL
(Section~\ref{sec:worth-headline}), AWWL + EMA (Holm
$p = \mrgArmAWWLemaHolm$) and the static wavelet arm (\mrgArmStaticHolm).
Objectives without a frequency term also have positive MRGs ($L_1$,
Charbonnier, Min-SNR-$\gamma$ and, not significantly, Huber), as does,
outside the family, the timestep-only arm of Section~\ref{sec:confound}
($\mathrm{MRG} = \mrgTimestep$ $[\mrgTimestepLo, \mrgTimestepHi]$,
$V = \valueTimestep$), whose gap to MSE is almost all MRG. After Holm
correction the MRG verdicts coincide with those on the FID gap to MSE
(Table~\ref{tab:compare-fid}), so across arms the test adds the
decomposition, what each arm's change is worth and where, not a more powerful
test of the gap. It also separates changes that band means do not: the
high-band part of Min-SNR-$\gamma$'s corner-only change
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}) is worth $\priceMinSNRHighValue$, against
AWWL's $\priceTransplantHighValue$.

\paragraph{A second member of the family.}
\label{sec:second-member}
Focal Frequency Loss (FFL; Section~\ref{sec:family-ffl}), which differs from
AWWL in its basis (global rather than spatially localised) and in its weights
(keyed on the residual rather than on $\sigma_t$), was trained untuned
($a = 1$, five seeds) as the sole objective on the $\epsilon$-residual, not
added to a spatial loss as its authors do. It is worse than MSE by
$+\dFFL$~FID (Holm $p = \dFFLHolm$; Table~\ref{tab:compare-fid}), in
precision but not in recall (Table~\ref{tab:compare-other}). It removes nearly all of the
high-band deficit and overshoots the reference in the low and mid bands
(Table~\ref{tab:bands}), a change worth $V = \valueArmFFL$
$[\valueArmFFLLo, \valueArmFFLHi]$, the largest positive $V$ of any trained
arm; yet its MRG is $\mrgArmFFL$ $[\mrgArmFFLLo, \mrgArmFFLHi]$ (Holm
$p = \mrgArmFFLHolm$). A positive MRG therefore arises without AWWL's
schedule and without a partial correction; FFL was, however, trained in a
form its authors did not use.

\subsection{A second feature space}
\label{sec:worth-dino}
Every value above is measured in Inception-V3 features. We repeated the main
measurements in DINOv2 ViT-L/14 features \citep{oquab2024dinov2}, the space
recommended by \citet{stein2023exposing}, as a Fr\'echet distance
FD$_{\text{DINOv2}}$ on the same samples, scored in floating point
(Appendix~\ref{app:scoring}; Table~\ref{tab:features}). The verdict on AWWL
becomes sharper. Its gap to MSE, unresolved in FID, is resolved:
$+\fddGapAWWL$ $[\fddGapAWWLLo, \fddGapAWWLHi]$ against a baseline of
\fddMSEmean. Its spectral change is worth nothing in this space
($V = \fddValueAWWL$ $[\fddValueAWWLLo, \fddValueAWWLHi]$), so the whole gap
is MRG ($+\fddMrgAWWL$, $p~\fddMrgAWWLP$); the mid third alone again carries
positive value ($+\fddValueMid$, against $+\fddValueHigh$ for the high third),
and in this space the band values do not add. Hazard H1 does not carry over:
white noise raises FD$_{\text{DINOv2}}$ ($\fddNoiseHalfValue$ at $\pm0.5$~LSB,
$\fddNoiseOneValue$ at $\pm1$~LSB), so the gain it buys in FID is a property of
Inception features at this operating point. On CelebA-64 both spaces agree:
the full radial match raises the distance ($\fddCelebaMatchValue$) and noise
lowers it ($+\fddCelebaNoiseHalfValue$). The positive control returns a
negative MRG in both spaces ($\fddPosOptMRG$, $p~\fddPosOptMRGP$), and the
two components of the two-by-two design are each worse than MSE in both
(timestep only $+\fddGapTimestep$, shape at constant $S$ $+\fddGapSumFixed$).
The value of a spectral change is therefore a property of the metric's
feature space as well as of the operating point, as precondition P4
anticipates; the conclusion that AWWL loses outside its mechanism holds in
both spaces and is stronger in the one the evaluation literature now
recommends.
